A trade blotter is the running record of an account's or a desk's orders
and trades: the time, instrument, side, quantity, price and status of
each, kept current while the market moves. The picture above is one, and
it runs: an order placed in the ticket fills against the quotes and joins
the list. Every price is simulated and no order leaves the page.

## Fill it yourself

- **Alt+B** or **Alt+S** sets the ticket to buy or to sell the selected
  instrument, and **Alt+Enter** submits it. A market order fills at once,
  a buy at the ask and a sell at the bid; a limit order stays *Working*
  until a quote reaches its price.
- **Ctrl+2** shows the orders, newest first. **Ctrl+1** shows the
  positions with their market value and profit and loss at the live
  prices.

## What makes it a live blotter

Both lists are [gpui-kit-grid](../) grids: a data grid written in Rust for
GPUI applications, here compiled to WebAssembly and drawn with WebGPU.

- Rows are [addressed by key](../concepts/), so a fill updates its order
  in place, and the selection stays on that order when the list re-sorts.
- A cell that changes [flashes](../guides/live-changes/), up or down.
- Sorting, filtering and [grouping with totals](../guides/grouping/) run
  in the background, at [thousands of updates a second](../performance/).

## On the desktop

Two of the repository's [examples](../examples/#native-only) are blotters
in a native window: `blotter`, with grouping, a totals row and pinned
columns, and `orders`, edited in place while the market streams in.

```sh
cargo run --release -p gpui-kit-grid --example blotter
cargo run --release -p gpui-kit-grid --example orders
```
